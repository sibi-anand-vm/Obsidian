### 3. Relationship Between Power Gain and Directive Gain

- **Directive Gain (DD)**: Measures the antenna's ability to focus energy in a specific direction compared to an isotropic radiator.
    
- **Power Gain (GG)**: Includes both the directive gain and the antenna's efficiency (ηη).

The relationship is:

G=η⋅D

**Answer:** Power gain is the product of antenna efficiency and directive gain.
### 4. Lobe Definition and Radiation Pattern Parts

**Lobe**: A region of the radiation pattern where the signal strength is locally maximized.

**Parts of Radiation Pattern:**

1. **Main Lobe**: The direction of maximum radiation.
    
2. **Side Lobes**: Smaller lobes adjacent to the main lobe.
    
3. **Back Lobe**: A lobe in the opposite direction of the main lobe.
    
4. **Nulls**: Directions where radiation is minimized.

10.**Basic Types of Radiation Patterns**

- **Omnidirectional Pattern**: Equal radiation in all directions in one plane (e.g., dipole antenna).
- **Directional Pattern**: Strong radiation in a particular direction (e.g., horn antenna).
- **Isotropic Pattern**: Hypothetical antenna with uniform radiation in all directions.
- **Lobed Pattern**: Consists of main lobe, side lobes, and back lobes.

12.**Beamwidth**: The angular width of the main lobe of the radiation pattern, measured between the half-power points (where the power is 50% of the maximum).

**Types of Beamwidth**:

1. **Half-Power Beamwidth (HPBW)**:
    
    - The angle between the points where the radiation intensity drops to half its maximum value.
        
    - Most commonly used measure of beamwidth.
        
2. **First-Null Beamwidth (FNBW)**:
    
    - The angle between the first nulls on either side of the main lobe.
        
3. **10-dB Beamwidth**:
    
    - The angle where the radiation intensity drops to 10% of its maximum value.
