Rezaee, A. A., & Akbari, M. (2025) proposed a multi-channel queue model designed to optimize service levels and staff availability in the banking sector. Their research focuses on balancing customer service efficiency and employee satisfaction by employing a multi-objective optimization approach that minimizes customer waiting time, reduces the number of assigned employees, and maximizes employee preferred working hours. Using a Poisson distribution to model customer arrival and service patterns, their meta-heuristic NSGA-II algorithm demonstrated an 8% reduction in waiting times and a 53% boost in employee satisfaction based on real data from Agribank, Iran. However, the study’s complexity due to the NP-hard nature of the scheduling problem requires computationally intensive algorithms, and it primarily addresses staffing optimization without direct integration of real-time sensor data or AI-based predictive queue management, which could limit adaptability in dynamic, real-world queue environments.

**Taton, T. K., Saha, B., Islam, M. J., & Mostaque, S. K. (2025)** proposed a comprehensive AI-based approach for queue waiting time prediction using ensemble tree methods combined with data balancing and explainable AI techniques. The study's main objective was to accurately forecast exact waiting times in bank teller queues framed as a regression problem, employing sophisticated preprocessing, feature selection, and dimensionality reduction methods. They utilized models such as Extra Trees, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting, integrating SMOTE to address class imbalance for classification tasks. Explainability was achieved through LIME and SHAP to interpret feature importance. The Extra Trees regressor yielded the best performance with an R² of 0.946 and low prediction errors. Key factors influencing waiting times included queue length, arrival time, and cashier identity. Limitations noted involve incomplete dataset metadata and ambiguous categorical labels, which constrain wider applicability and model interpretability.

Their proposed system uses predictive analytics for wait time estimation and incorporates automated scheduling, dynamic resource adjustment, and patient prioritization algorithms. These factors can limit scalability and flexible deployment in resource-constrained or rural hospital settings

Vidhya, V., Palanivel, A., & Kumar, V. R. (2025) proposed a health checkup queue management system that integrates hybrid scheduling algorithms, combining First-Come-First-Serve and Fisher-Yates shuffle methods, with real-time monitoring to optimize patient flow and resource utilization. The system schedules patients differently based on age categories and offers a mobile application providing real-time queue updates and navigation assistance. Experimental evaluations demonstrated a 40% reduction in waiting times and a 35% improvement in service turnaround. Despite these gains, the approach faces challenges with potential user adoption and interface usability. Additionally, the system currently lacks AI-driven predictive capabilities for dynamic patient volume forecasting. This limits its adaptability to sudden demand fluctuations. Future enhancements aim to incorporate machine learning techniques for adaptive and data-driven scheduling improvements.

**Patki, S., Pachpande, D., & Thite, S. (2020)** proposed a smart queue management system utilizing deep neural networks (DNN) and convolutional neural networks (CNN) for real-time queue monitoring and prediction. The system captures video input processed through CNN for accurate person detection and counting, while a DNN predicts queue build-up and advises on counter availability. Their approach integrates image processing with machine learning to pre-emptively open additional service counters. Experimental results demonstrate high object detection accuracy (~95%) and effective real-time queue predictions. However, the solution requires adequate camera coverage and computational resources, which could limit deployment scalability in resource-constrained or large-scale environments. The system currently lacks integration with customer notification mechanisms such as SMS or mobile apps for enhanced user experience.

**Tokala Sreeja, Pravalika, Slycee Leyona, and Saranya (2025)** proposed QTrack, a web-based smart queue management system aimed at transforming traditional queuing by enabling remote queue registration and real-time queue position tracking through digital interfaces. The system leverages modern web technologies such as Node.js, MongoDB, and WebSockets to provide real-time updates and automated notifications via SMS, email, or app alerts, significantly reducing waiting times and physical crowding. The platform supports features like queue cancellation, rescheduling, and integrated billing, making it suitable for hospitals, banks, and academic institutions. Experimental outcomes highlight improvements in operational efficiency, transparency, and user convenience. However, the system’s limitations include dependency on reliable internet connectivity and potential challenges in adoption by less tech-savvy users, as well as scalability concerns in extremely high-demand environments without additional sensor-based automation or AI-driven predictive features.

The paper details several application scenarios, including hospital queue management and network congestion control, highlighting the system's adaptability and predictive analytics for demand forecasting. However, the study's broad focus leads to limited empirical validation for sector-specific customization; the reliance on simulated testing means challenges related to real-world deployment—such as adaptation to unique user behaviors or infrastructure constraints—require further exploration to ensure scalability and context-specific effectiveness.
